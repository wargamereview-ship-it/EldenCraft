# EldenCraft 0.4 plan

Written 6 October 2026 from the planning questions. Nothing here is built yet. One release when all six are done
(branch `0.4-dev`; the mod version is already 0.4.0). Mark a feature done only once it has been played.

## Status: built, not yet played

All six are in `0.4-dev`, built and unit-tested but **not yet played in the game**. What each is, and where it differs from the plan:

1. **Combat balance** (`CombatBalance`, `native_damage.rs`): player damage lands at 60% in tier 1 down to 34% in tier 7 (`CombatBalance.DAMAGE_BY_TIER`), and
   poise damage per weapon class is about half what it was, so only crits, sprint hits, Knockback and the mace smash break poise. Tune from play.
2. **Starter loop** (`ResourceRules`): the real blocker was that tier 1 mines gave only copper and coal. Tier 1 now gives iron 40% / copper 35% / coal 25%,
   and tier 1 rocks give raw copper or raw iron in equal shares. The 30-minute walk-through is still to do.
3. **Death** (`XpPile`, `XpMath`): XP levels are cleared on respawn and spill as one pile (the last solid ground if the death was a fall); collect it once;
   a second death before that replaces it. Saved with the world. Unlike the plan, there is **no timeout** on a pile: it waits until collected or replaced.
4. **Set passives** (`ArmourSet`, `SetPassives`): all 17, two pieces for the passive, four for +1 on every ward worn. Some drafts changed because they need
   native sound or hit hooks: Wayfarer's Hide is +8% speed (not quieter steps), Nameless Oath is faster sneaking (not silent steps), Carian Lakeguard is armour
   toughness, Redmane Exile is fire resistance (no ignite-on-hit), Bloodroot Sovereign heals on a kill (not on bleed), Ashen Crucible gives Strength after a kill.
   New set pieces carry a tooltip line; the chat announces when a set bonus starts or stops.
5. **Traders** (`merchants.rs`, `Merchants`, `MerchantTrades`): 51 NpcParam rows (Kalé and the nomadic, isolated, hermit, abandoned and imprisoned merchants).
   Hewg and Iji are **not** replaced: they have quests, so they stay as the real, visible characters and are talked to with R.
   Hewg's upgrade tab is replaced by Minecraft's anvil: R within 3.5 blocks of him opens it (`HewgAnvil`), Shift+R still talks to him natively. The tab itself cannot be
   intercepted (no way to see or hook the open native menu from the SDK), so this works on the key press, not inside his dialogue. Iji is left fully native.
   Roderika works the same way: R within 3.5 blocks opens a shop of spawn eggs (wolf, fox, cat, parrot, donkey, horse, llama, allay), Shift+R talks natively.
   Spawned mobs should stand on the terrain: `BlockCollisionsMixin` adds Elden Ring's collision, as a voxel skin, to the normal block collision of every entity
   (only the local player uses the exact-triangle collider on top). Two things to watch in play: mobs are coarser than the player on slopes, and a mob that wanders
   beyond the scanned area around the player has no floor and will fall. Each is hidden natively and a villager stands in its place; using it opens a chest-style screen of offers for the region's tier, paid in levels,
   with limited uses that refill at a grace rest. The Shadow of the Erdtree traders are only covered where they share these rows; the rest need their rows added.
   The trade tables in `MerchantTrades` are a starting point to tune.
6. **Interactions**: nothing was changed; this is the test pass.

## Decisions

| # | Feature | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Starter loop | Iron kit in about 30 minutes, around Limgrave, pure Minecraft progression |
| 2 | Death | Lose **XP levels only**. All XP spills in one recovery pile at the death spot; collect it once to get it back; dying again before that loses the old pile (like runes) |
| 3 | Interactions | Nothing has blocked play yet. Fix only what a test pass finds |
| 4 | Traders | **Every** open-world merchant, base game **and** Shadow of the Erdtree, becomes a Minecraft villager body with a tiered, regional trade table. Paid in **XP levels** ("runes"). Fixed table; uses restock after a grace rest |
| 5 | Combat | Player damage **scaled by region tier**; stagger by **poise by enemy family** like Elden Ring. Today everything staggers too easily and the player hits too hard |
| 6 | Set bonuses | **All 17 sets, each with a unique themed passive**, plus a ward boost |

## Build order

1. **Combat balance (5)** and the **starter loop (1)** together. They shape everything after.
2. **Death and XP recovery (2).** Also settles what XP is for, which traders need.
3. **Set passives (6).**
4. **Traders (4).** Depends on XP being a currency and on tiers.
5. **Interactions (3).** A test pass; fix what it finds.

After each feature, build the DLL and JAR and play it before starting the next. Final packaging only at the end.

## 1 + 5. Combat balance and the starter loop

**What the player sees:** an early enemy takes a believable number of hits with an iron sword; a later enemy takes
many more; big enemies stagger on a heavy hit or a crit, not every swing.

**Damage (code: `game/src/combat.rs`, `HP_PER_DAMAGE = 25`).** Today every Minecraft damage point removes 25 Elden Ring
HP regardless of target. Make the conversion depend on the enemy's region tier, so the same weapon feels weaker against
later enemies. The tier table already exists in Java (`LootRules.mapTier`, from `loot_rules.json`); the damage is applied in
Rust. Decide at build time whether Java sends the tier with each actor, or Rust derives it from the enemy's max HP.
Start values come from play: aim for an iron sword killing a starting-area soldier in roughly 3-5 hits and a
Tier 3 enemy in roughly 8-12.

**Stagger (code: `game/src/native_damage.rs`).** Poise damage per weapon class (`atk_super_armor`) and the reaction size are set
there. Lower the poise damage so only heavy swings, crits, sprint hits and Knockback break an enemy's poise, and let
each enemy's own Elden Ring poise decide the rest (small enemies flinch, big ones rarely do). Keep the Knockback
enchantment as the stagger lever.

**Starter loop.** Fresh world, empty inventory: logs and stone by hand, workbench, wooden pickaxe, furnace, copper then
iron, smelt, iron pickaxe and sword and a few armour pieces, within about 30 minutes. Walk it end to end and fix every
dead end (missing recipe, too few nodes, ore too far). Check a placed block survives leaving and returning.

**Done when:** an empty character reaches the iron kit in about 30 minutes without a reset, and early and late
enemies feel clearly different to fight.

## 2. Death and XP recovery

**What the player sees:** on death, their XP levels become a pile where they fell. They respawn at the grace, walk back
and collect it. Die again first and the old pile is gone.

**Rules to settle while building:**
- Only XP is lost; inventory, hotbar and armour are kept (today's behaviour).
- One pile at a time per world. A new death replaces the old pile.
- A pile that cannot be reached (fell into a void, inside geometry): move it to the nearest safe floor, and give up
  and keep the XP after a set time rather than losing it unfairly.
- The pile must survive leaving and reloading the world, and a grace respawn into another map.
- Mending and enchanting use XP too. Show the pile and the amount clearly so it never feels like a bug.

**Code:** the native death flow is in `game/src/lib.rs` and `combat.rs`; XP is Minecraft's, so the drop, the pile and
saving it belong on the Java side next to `SkyCombat` and `RewardProgress`.

**Done when:** you can die, run back and recover the levels; a second death before pickup loses the first pile; and
none of this breaks the grace respawn.

Each set also raises its ward by one level. These are **drafts for you to change**; the effects favour things the game
can already do (movement, noise, healing, status, fire).

|---|---|

| Morne Wanderer (1) | Move faster in water and breathe longer |
| Carian Lakeguard (2) | Spell and arrow damage reduced; Glintstone ward +1 |
| Stormgate Vanguard (2) | Knockback resistance; Storm ward +1 |
| Amber Roadwarden (3) | Slow healing while standing still |
| Redmane Exile (3) | Fire resistance; hits that land set the attacker alight (cooldown) |
| Eternal Pathfinder (3) | Nearby hostile enemies glow through walls |

**Done when:** each set's passive can be switched on by wearing it and is visible in the tooltip.

## 4. Traders (the big one)

**What the player sees:** where Elden Ring has an open-world merchant (Kalé, the nomadic merchants, the Roundtable
smith and the Shadow of the Erdtree traders), a Minecraft villager body stands in its place, labelled with the
merchant's name. Talking to it opens a trade screen with a table matched to the region's tier. Trades are paid in XP
levels, have limited uses, and refill after a grace rest.

**Rules to settle:**
- **Which merchants.** Build the list from NpcParam (the names table already identifies them, for example "Merchant Kalé")
  and review it with you before deleting any native shop.
- **The Elden Ring merchant.** Hidden while the villager stands in. Decide whether a native quest or dialogue still needs
  the original (Kalé, Hewg, Patches and so on) before hiding it.
- **Trade tables by tier.** Tier 1 (basic tools, food, a few materials) through tier 7 (late DLC gear), priced in levels.
  Keep prices low enough that the death rule stays fair and high enough that levels still matter.
- **XP as the price.** Vanilla villager trades are item-for-item, so this needs a custom trade menu that deducts
  levels. Spending levels competes with enchanting and Mending; settle caps so a player cannot spend themselves out of
  enchanting.
- **Restock.** Reuse the grace-rest hook that resets gathering nodes.

**Code:** the actor table in `game/src/combat.rs` currently carries hostile enemies only; friendly merchants need to
be published with their own flag and position, and the Java side spawns villager-style entities for them (as
`SkyrimActorEntity` does for enemies). The trade menu and table are new Java. Persisted per world.

**Risks:** the largest feature by far. Merchants moving or despawning in Elden Ring, merchants inside scripted
quest areas, and every region and DLC tier all need testing. Build one merchant end to end first (Kalé), then the rest.

**Done when:** every listed merchant is replaced, its trades are tiered to its region, paying in levels works, restock
works, and none of them traps the player or breaks the native world.

## 3. Interactions

No interaction has blocked you yet, so this is a test pass: climb ladders, use lift switches, open chests and
corpses, use graces, doors and levers under Minecraft control, in a few regions. Fix only what fails. **Done when:**
the pass finds nothing, or what it found is fixed.

## Cross-cutting

- **XP is now a currency and a penalty.** Mending, enchanting, traders and death all touch it. After the death and
  trader work, play a session specifically to check XP feels fair.
- **Tiers drive three systems** (loot, damage, trade tables). Keep one tier source of truth.
- **Native Windows depth capture** and the status buildup display were fixed this week; they still need a play-check
  alongside this work.
- **Release:** one release at the end. The `0.4-preview` branch is not needed for this plan.
